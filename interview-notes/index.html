Meta questions (about the interview itself)

Q1What are interviewers really scoring in a coding round?GoogleMetaAtlassian

Answer: Four signals, almost everywhere:

  1. Problem solving — did you find an efficient approach and can you justify it (complexity, why it is correct)?
  2. Coding — readable, idiomatic, well-named, decomposed into helpers.
  3. Verification — you test your own code: dry run, edge cases, fix bugs before the interviewer points them out.
  4. Communication — you think aloud, clarify, accept hints and explain trade-offs.
💡 A correct solution with silent coding and no testing often scores lower than a slightly slower solution that was communicated and verified well.
Q2I have 8+ years of experience. How is my loop different from a fresher's?All T1All T2
  • Fewer pure-DSA rounds, more design — usually 1–2 coding, 1–2 system design, 1 LLD/machine coding (India), 1 behavioral/leadership, 1 hiring-manager round.
  • Behavioral weight goes up — scope, influence without authority, mentoring, handling ambiguity, conflict with senior stakeholders.
  • Down-levelling risk — a strong coding round with a weak design round often turns a Senior (L5/E5/SDE3) loop into an L4/SDE2 offer.
  • Project deep dive — expect 30–45 min on "the most complex system you built": numbers, failure modes, what you would change.
⚠️ Trap: preparing only LeetCode. At senior level, design + behavioral decide the level; coding only clears the bar.
Q3How many problems should I solve, and which ones?🔥 hot

Answer: Patterns beat counts. ~150 well-understood problems (Blind 75 / NeetCode 150 style) across ~15 patterns is enough for most loops if you can re-solve them cold. Track for each: the pattern, the key insight in one line, and the bug you made.

💡 Rule: if you could not solve it in 25 min, read the solution, then re-solve from scratch after 1, 3 and 7 days (spaced repetition).
Q4What changed in 2025–26 interviews because of AI?MetaGoogleMicrosoft
  • Meta began piloting an AI-enabled coding round (Oct 2025, expanding through 2026): 60 min in CoderPad with a built-in assistant, a multi-file codebase where you build, extend or debug — instead of two puzzles.
  • Google brought back at least one in-person round for many roles (to counter AI cheating), and has reportedly been piloting an AI-assisted code-comprehension round for some junior/mid roles.
  • Microsoft candidates have reported an AI-assisted coding round for some AI teams.
  • Everywhere else: remote OAs are more proctored; follow-up "explain this line" questions are more common.
↪ Formats are pilots and vary by team/level — always ask your recruiter "what is the exact loop, and is any round AI-assisted?" See page 16 for how to perform in AI rounds.

Clear the coding bar with patterns (pages 3–6, 19–20), win the level with design (pages 10–13, 21–22), and win the offer with stories (pages 17–18, 24).

Loop questions you should be able to answer

Q1What is a Bar Raiser and how do I "pass" one?Amazon

Answer: A trained interviewer from outside the hiring team whose job is to ensure every hire is better than 50% of current people at that level. They usually probe Leadership Principles deeply and can veto.

  • Expect 3–5 follow-ups per story: "What exactly did you do?", "What data?", "What would you do differently?"
  • Use real numbers (latency, cost, % improvement, team size, dates).
  • Have 2 stories per principle so you never repeat one in the same loop.
⚠️ "We decided…" without your own action = weak signal. Say "I".
Q2How does Google's hiring committee and team match work?Google

Answer: Interviewers write detailed feedback + a score. A hiring committee (people who did not interview you) reads the packet and decides hire/no-hire and level. Then you go through team matching (calls with managers) before the final offer and comp review.

💡 Because strangers read your packet, the interviewer must be able to write down your reasoning — think aloud, state complexity, name your test cases.
Q3What is a machine coding round and how is it graded?FlipkartUberSwiggyRazorpay

Answer: You get a problem statement (e.g. "Splitwise", "parking lot", "food ordering") and ~90–120 minutes to build a working, runnable program — usually in-memory, no DB/UI. Graded on:

  1. It runs and handles the main flows + edge cases (demo via a driver/main or tests).
  2. Modelling — right entities, clear responsibilities, no god class.
  3. Extensibility — new requirement (new pricing rule, new split type) added without rewriting: Strategy/Factory, interfaces.
  4. Code hygiene — naming, packages/folders, exceptions, no hard-coding.
  5. Concurrency awareness when relevant (double booking).
↪ Full walkthroughs on page 11.
Q4What are Atlassian's values, and how do I prepare for the values round?Atlassian

Answer: Five values:

  1. Open company, no bullshit — transparency, candid feedback (e.g. "feedback to a senior stakeholder").
  2. Build with heart and balance — quality vs speed trade-offs, sustainable pace.
  3. Don't #@!% the customer — customer impact first.
  4. Play, as a team — collaboration, helping others succeed.
  5. Be the change you seek — you fixed a broken process yourself.

Prepare 1–2 STAR stories per value; the interviewer is usually outside the team, so explain context simply.

Q5What do service companies ask experienced (lateral) candidates?TCSInfosysAccentureCognizant
  • Technical: your stack in depth (C#/.NET: DI, middleware, async, EF Core; SQL joins/indexes; REST; Azure/AWS; Docker), plus 1–2 easy/medium coding questions.
  • Project: architecture diagram of your current project, your role, a production incident you handled.
  • Scenario: "API is slow in production — what do you do?", "Client changes requirement mid-sprint?"
  • Managerial: estimation, team handling, conflict, Agile ceremonies, client communication.
  • HR: current/expected CTC, notice period, relocation, reason for change.

  • Exact rounds, order and duration; which ones are elimination rounds?
  • Language allowed? Code executed or not? Any AI-assisted round?
  • Which level am I being considered for? Is system design 1 or 2 rounds?
  • For machine coding: IDE allowed? Internet? Expected deliverable (main method / tests)?
  • Can I reschedule if I need one more week of prep? (Almost always yes.)

Hashing & prefix sums

Q1Two Sum — return indices of two numbers that add to target.🔥AmazonGoogleAdobeTCSInfosys

Answer: Walk once; for each x check whether target - x was seen. Store value → index after the check (so an element is not paired with itself). O(n) time, O(n) space.

var seen = new Dictionary<int, int>();
for (int i = 0; i < nums.Length; i++) {
    if (seen.TryGetValue(target - nums[i], out var j)) return new[] { j, i };
    seen[nums[i]] = i;
}
return Array.Empty<int>();
↪ Sorted input? → two pointers, O(1) space. All pairs / count pairs? → map of counts. Stream of numbers? → keep the map, answer per arrival.
Q2Subarray Sum Equals K (array has negatives).🔥MetaGoogleAtlassian

Answer: sum(i..j) = prefix[j] − prefix[i−1]. So at each position, the number of subarrays ending here with sum k = how many earlier prefixes equal prefix - k. Seed the map with {0: 1} (the empty prefix). O(n) / O(n).

var count = new Dictionary<int, int> { [0] = 1 };
int sum = 0, ans = 0;
foreach (var x in nums) {
    sum += x;
    ans += count.GetValueOrDefault(sum - k);
    count[sum] = count.GetValueOrDefault(sum) + 1;
}
⚠️ Sliding window does not work here — negatives break the "shrink when too big" rule. Say this explicitly; interviewers love it.
Q3Product of Array Except Self — without division, O(n).AmazonMetaMicrosoftAdobe

Answer: answer[i] = (product of everything left of i) × (product of everything right of i). Pass 1 fills left products into the output; pass 2 walks from the right with a running suffix product. O(n) time, O(1) extra (output array not counted).

var res = new int[n]; res[0] = 1;
for (int i = 1; i < n; i++) res[i] = res[i - 1] * nums[i - 1];
int right = 1;
for (int i = n - 1; i >= 0; i--) { res[i] *= right; right *= nums[i]; }
↪ "Why not divide?" — zeros (one zero / two zeros cases) and overflow. Mention both.
Q4Longest Consecutive Sequence in O(n).GoogleAmazonWalmart

Answer: Put all numbers in a HashSet. Only start counting from x when x - 1 is not in the set (x is a sequence start), then walk x+1, x+2… Each number is visited at most twice → O(n).

⚠️ Sorting gives O(n log n) — fine as a first answer, but the question says O(n).
Q5Group Anagrams.AmazonMetaSalesforceCognizant

Answer: Map a canonical key → list of words. Key = sorted letters (O(k log k) per word) or a 26-count signature like "1#0#2#…" (O(k)). Total O(n·k) with counts.

↪ Unicode input? → use Dictionary<char,int> signature or sorted string, not a 26-array.
3

Kadane, stock & two pointers

Q6Maximum Subarray (Kadane's algorithm).🔥MicrosoftAmazonTCSWipro

Answer: At each element decide: extend the previous subarray or restart here — cur = max(x, cur + x). Track the best. O(n) / O(1). Works for all-negative arrays (answer = largest element).

int best = nums[0], cur = 0;
foreach (var x in nums) {
    cur = Math.Max(x, cur + x);   // extend or restart
    best = Math.Max(best, cur);
}
↪ Return the indices too → remember start when you restart. Max product subarray → track both max and min (a negative flips them).
Q7Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock (one transaction).AmazonMetaFlipkartAccenture

Answer: Track the minimum price so far; profit if sold today = price − min. Keep the max. O(n) / O(1).

↪ Unlimited transactions → sum every positive day-to-day rise (greedy). At most k transactions / cooldown / fee → DP with states (holding, not holding).
Q83Sum — all unique triplets summing to 0.🔥MetaAmazonAdobe

Answer: Sort. For each i (skip if same as previous), run two pointers L = i+1, R = end: sum < 0 → L++, sum > 0 → R--, else record and move both, skipping duplicates. O(n²) time, O(1) extra (ignoring sort).

⚠️ The duplicate-skipping is the whole question — do it for i, L and R. Early exit when nums[i] > 0.
Q9Trapping Rain Water.🔥GoogleAmazonUberAdobe

Answer: Water above i = min(maxLeft, maxRight) − height[i]. Two pointers: move the side with the smaller max, because that side's water is already decided by its own max. O(n) / O(1).

int L = 0, R = h.Length - 1, lMax = 0, rMax = 0, water = 0;
while (L < R) {
    if (h[L] < h[R]) { lMax = Math.Max(lMax, h[L]); water += lMax - h[L]; L++; }
    else            { rMax = Math.Max(rMax, h[R]); water += rMax - h[R]; R--; }
}
↪ Alternatives to mention: prefix/suffix max arrays (O(n) space), monotonic stack (counts water layer by layer). Container With Most Water is the cousin: move the shorter wall.
Q10Sort Colors (0s, 1s, 2s) in one pass.MicrosoftTCSInfosysCapgemini

Answer: Dutch National Flag: pointers lo, mid, hi. 0 → swap with lo, lo++, mid++. 1 → mid++. 2 → swap with hi, hi-- (do not advance mid — the swapped-in value is unchecked). O(n) / O(1).

Sliding window

Q11Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters.🔥AmazonGoogleFlipkartInfosys

Answer: Window [L, R]. Remember last index of each char; when s[R] was seen inside the window, jump L past it. O(n) time, O(alphabet) space.

var last = new Dictionary<char, int>();
int L = 0, best = 0;
for (int R = 0; R < s.Length; R++) {
    if (last.TryGetValue(s[R], out var p) && p >= L) L = p + 1;  // p >= L: only if inside window
    last[s[R]] = R;
    best = Math.Max(best, R - L + 1);
}
Q12Minimum Window Substring (contains all chars of t).MetaGoogleUberAtlassian

Answer: Count what we need. Grow R; when a needed char arrives, missing--. While missing == 0 the window is valid → record, then shrink L; if removing s[L] makes it needed again, missing++. O(|s| + |t|).

var need = new int[128]; foreach (var c in t) need[c]++;
int missing = t.Length, L = 0, bestL = 0, bestLen = int.MaxValue;
for (int R = 0; R < s.Length; R++) {
    if (need[s[R]]-- > 0) missing--;
    while (missing == 0) {
        if (R - L + 1 < bestLen) { bestLen = R - L + 1; bestL = L; }
        if (++need[s[L++]] > 0) missing++;
    }
}
return bestLen == int.MaxValue ? "" : s.Substring(bestL, bestLen);
💡 Generic window template: grow R → while invalid (or valid, for "minimum") shrink L → update answer. Longest = update after shrinking; shortest = update while shrinking.
Q13Longest Repeating Character Replacement (at most k changes).GoogleUberAtlassian

Answer: Window is valid if windowLen - maxFreq ≤ k (chars we must change). Grow R, update counts and maxFreq; if invalid, move L by one. O(n). maxFreq never needs to decrease — the answer only grows when a bigger maxFreq appears.

Bugs interviewers see every day
  • Forgetting {0: 1} in prefix-sum counting.
  • Using sliding window when the array has negatives.
  • Not skipping duplicates in 3Sum (or skipping too early and losing answers).
  • Off-by-one in window length: it is R - L + 1.
  • int overflow on sums/products — mention long.

Linked lists

Q1Reverse a linked list — iteratively and recursively.🔥MicrosoftAmazonTCSInfosysWipro

Answer: Iterative: three pointers prev, cur, next; flip one arrow per step. O(n) / O(1). Recursive: reverse the rest, then make head.next.next = head, head.next = null. O(n) stack.

ListNode prev = null, cur = head;
while (cur != null) {
    var next = cur.next;   // 1. save
    cur.next = prev;       // 2. flip
    prev = cur;            // 3. advance prev
    cur = next;            // 4. advance cur
}
return prev;
↪ Reverse in groups of k, reverse between positions m..n, palindrome list (reverse 2nd half, compare, restore).
Q2Detect a cycle and return the node where it starts. Why does Floyd's trick work?AmazonMicrosoftAdobeCognizant

Answer: slow moves 1, fast moves 2. If they meet, there is a cycle. Slow walked a + b; fast walked twice that, i.e. a full number of extra loops: a + b = k·L. So a = k·L − b = c + (k−1)·L — walking a steps from the head lands on the start, same as walking c steps from M. O(n) / O(1).

Q3Merge K sorted lists.🔥AmazonGoogleMetaUber

Answer: Min-heap holding the current head of each list. Pop smallest, append, push its next. N total nodes, k lists → O(N log k) time, O(k) space. Alternative: divide & conquer pairwise merge — same complexity, no heap.

var pq = new PriorityQueue<ListNode, int>();
foreach (var l in lists) if (l != null) pq.Enqueue(l, l.val);
var dummy = new ListNode(); var tail = dummy;
while (pq.TryDequeue(out var node, out _)) {
    tail = tail.next = node;
    if (node.next != null) pq.Enqueue(node.next, node.next.val);
}
return dummy.next;
Q4Remove N-th node from end in one pass?MetaTCS
Dummy head; move fast n+1 steps, then move both until fast is null; slow.next = slow.next.next. Dummy handles removing the head.
Q5Intersection of two lists?AmazonAdobe
Two pointers; when one reaches the end, jump to the other list's head. Both travel a + b + c and meet at the intersection (or both null).
Q6Copy list with random pointer?MetaAmazon
Map old → new (O(n) space), or interleave clones A→A'→B→B', set A'.random = A.random.next, then unweave (O(1) extra).
Q7Design an LRU cache?🔥AmazonAtlassianSalesforce
HashMap key → node + doubly linked list with dummy head/tail; touch = move to head, evict = tail.prev. All O(1). Full C# in DSA notes p.5. Thread-safe? → lock around ops or striped locks.

Stacks & monotonic stacks

Q8Valid Parentheses.🔥AmazonMetaTCSAccenture

Answer: Push openers; on a closer, the top must be its matching opener, else false. At the end the stack must be empty. O(n). Quick reject: odd length.

↪ Min removals to make valid (Meta favourite): count unmatched ")" while scanning and unmatched "(" left over — remove those indices.
Q9Daily Temperatures / Next Greater Element.GoogleAmazonFlipkart

Answer: Keep a stack of indices with decreasing temperatures. For each day, pop while today is warmer — each popped day's answer is i - popped. Each index is pushed and popped once → O(n).

var st = new Stack<int>(); var ans = new int[t.Length];
for (int i = 0; i < t.Length; i++) {
    while (st.Count > 0 && t[i] > t[st.Peek()]) { int j = st.Pop(); ans[j] = i - j; }
    st.Push(i);
}
↪ Same pattern: Largest Rectangle in Histogram, Stock Span, Remove K Digits, Sum of Subarray Minimums.
Q10Design a Min Stack (getMin in O(1)).AmazonWalmartInfosys

Answer: Store pairs (value, minSoFar) on one stack, or keep a second stack that pushes when a value ≤ current min. All ops O(1).

Binary search

Q11Search in Rotated Sorted Array.🔥MetaMicrosoftAdobePayPal

Answer: One half around mid is always sorted. If nums[lo] ≤ nums[mid] the left half is sorted: target in [lo, mid) → go left, else right. Otherwise the right half is sorted — mirror it. O(log n).

⚠️ With duplicates, nums[lo] == nums[mid] is ambiguous → lo++; worst case becomes O(n). Say it.
Q12Koko Eating Bananas / Ship Packages in D Days — "binary search on the answer".GoogleAmazonFlipkart

Q1Diameter of a Binary Tree.🔥MetaGoogleAtlassian

Answer: DFS returns the node's height to its parent; while there, update a global best with left + right (the longest path that bends at this node). O(n) time, O(h) stack.

int best = 0;
int Height(TreeNode n) {
    if (n == null) return 0;
    int l = Height(n.left), r = Height(n.right);
    best = Math.Max(best, l + r);      // path through n (edges)
    return 1 + Math.Max(l, r);         // what the parent needs
}

Q3Lowest Common Ancestor — binary tree and BST.🔥MetaAmazonMicrosoftFlipkart

↪ Nodes have parent pointers → it becomes "intersection of two linked lists". p or q may not exist → count found nodes.

Q3House Robber (no two adjacent houses).AmazonGoogleCognizant

Q4Coin Change (fewest coins) vs Coin Change II (number of ways).🔥AmazonGooglePayPalWalmart

Fewest: dp[a] = min over coins c of dp[a - c] + 1, dp[0] = 0, unreachable = ∞ → return −1. O(A · C).

Ways: dp[0] = 1; outer loop coins, inner loop amounts → counts combinations (1+2 same as 2+1).

var dp = new int[amount + 1]; dp[0] = 1;
foreach (var c in coins)                 // coins OUTSIDE → combinations
    for (int a = c; a <= amount; a++)
        dp[a] += dp[a - c];
⚠️ Swap the loops (amount outside) and you count permutations — that is Combination Sum IV. Interviewers ask exactly this.
↪ Why not greedy (largest coin first)? Coins {1, 3, 4}, amount 6: greedy 4+1+1 = 3 coins; optimal 3+3 = 2.
Q5Longest Increasing Subsequence — O(n²) and O(n log n).🔥GoogleMicrosoftAtlassian

O(n²): dp[i] = 1 + max(dp[j]) for j < i with nums[j] < nums[i].

O(n log n): keep tails[k] = smallest possible tail of an increasing subsequence of length k+1. For each x, binary-search the first tail ≥ x and replace it (or append). Length of tails = answer.

var tails = new List<int>();
foreach (var x in nums) {
    int i = tails.BinarySearch(x);
    if (i < 0) i = ~i;                       // insertion point = first ≥ x
    if (i == tails.Count) tails.Add(x); else tails[i] = x;
}
return tails.Count;
⚠️ tails is not the actual subsequence. To print one, keep parent indices.

Backtracking

Q13Subsets, Permutations, Combination Sum — one template?🔥MetaAmazonUberInfosys

Answer: Choose → explore → un-choose.

void Backtrack(int start, List<int> path) {
    result.Add(new List<int>(path));          // subsets: record every node
    for (int i = start; i < nums.Length; i++) {
        if (i > start && nums[i] == nums[i - 1]) continue;  // skip dups (sorted)
        path.Add(nums[i]);                       // choose
        Backtrack(i + 1, path);                  // explore (i, not i+1, to reuse: Combination Sum)
        path.RemoveAt(path.Count - 1);           // un-choose
    }
}
  • Permutations: loop from 0 every time, use a used[] array; record only when path.Count == n. O(n · n!).
  • Combination Sum: recurse with i (reuse allowed), stop when remaining < 0; sort and break early when nums[i] > remaining.
Q14N-Queens and Word Search.AmazonMicrosoftAdobe

N-Queens: place row by row; keep sets for columns, r - c diagonals and r + c anti-diagonals for O(1) conflict checks.

Word Search: DFS from each cell matching word[0]; mark the cell (e.g. '#') before recursing and restore it after. O(R·C·4^L). Prune: if the board lacks enough letters, return false immediately.

Q2What happens during a context switch?MicrosoftAdobeWipro

Answer: Timer interrupt or blocking call → kernel saves the running thread's CPU state (registers, program counter, stack pointer) into its PCB/TCB → scheduler picks the next one → restores its state. Between processes it also switches page tables, so TLB/cache entries become cold — that's the hidden cost. Thread switches inside a process skip the address-space change.

Q3Virtual memory, paging, page faults and thrashing.MicrosoftGoogleTCSHCLTech
  • Virtual memory: each process sees a large private address space; the MMU maps virtual pages to physical frames via page tables. Enables isolation and running programs bigger than RAM.
  • Paging: fixed-size pages (often 4 KB). No external fragmentation; some internal fragmentation in the last page.
  • TLB: a cache of recent page-table translations — a TLB miss costs a page-table walk.
  • Page fault: page not in RAM → OS loads it from disk (major fault) and retries the instruction. Replacement policies: LRU (approximated by Clock), FIFO (Belady's anomaly), Optimal (theoretical).
  • Thrashing: working sets don't fit in RAM, so the system spends its time paging. Fix: reduce multiprogramming, add RAM, improve locality.
Q4Stack vs heap memory?TCSCognizant
Stack: per thread, LIFO frames, automatic, very fast, small (≈1 MB default for .NET threads) → stack overflow on deep recursion. Heap: shared, dynamic, managed by GC/allocator, slower, fragmentation.
Q5User mode vs kernel mode; system call?MicrosoftInfosys
Kernel mode can execute privileged instructions and touch hardware. A system call (read, write, fork) traps from user to kernel mode via a controlled entry point, then returns.
Q6CPU scheduling algorithms?TCSWipro
FCFS (convoy effect), SJF/SRTF (optimal average wait, starves long jobs), Round Robin (time slice; good response), Priority (starvation → fix with aging), Multilevel Feedback Queue (what real OSes approximate).

Synchronisation & deadlocks

Q8Mutex vs semaphore vs monitor.🔥MicrosoftAmazonSalesforceInfosys
  • Mutex: one owner at a time; must be released by the thread that acquired it. Protects a critical section.
  • Semaphore: a counter of permits (counting) — limits concurrency to N (e.g. max 10 DB calls). Any thread can signal. Binary semaphore ≈ lock without ownership.
  • Monitor: mutex + condition variables bundled with the data (C# lock + Monitor.Wait/Pulse, Java synchronized + wait/notify).
💡 One-liner: "A mutex is a key to one room; a semaphore is a bowl of N keys."
Q9What is a deadlock? Necessary conditions and how to prevent it.🔥MicrosoftAmazonPayPalTCSAccenture

Coffman conditions (all 4 needed): mutual exclusion, hold-and-wait, no preemption, circular wait. Break any one:

  • Circular wait → global lock ordering (most practical).
  • Hold-and-wait → acquire all locks at once, or TryEnter with timeout and back off.
  • No preemption → timeouts/cancellation.
  • Avoidance: Banker's algorithm (theory). Detection: wait-for graph + kill a victim (databases do this).
↪ Livelock = threads keep reacting to each other but make no progress (both step aside forever). Starvation = a thread never gets the resource (fix: fairness/aging).
Q10Race condition — example and three ways to fix it.AmazonAtlassianCognizant

Answer: count++ is read-modify-write; two threads read 5, both write 6 → lost update. Fixes:

  1. Lock around the critical section (lock (_gate) { count++; }).
  2. Atomic instruction — Interlocked.Increment(ref count) (compare-and-swap under the hood).
  3. Avoid sharing — thread-local counters merged at the end, or immutable data / message passing (channels, actors).
↪ Check-then-act races (if (!dict.ContainsKey(k)) dict.Add(k, v)) → ConcurrentDictionary.GetOrAdd. Same race exists in databases → unique constraints / conditional writes.
Q11Implement producer–consumer with a bounded buffer.MicrosoftUberSalesforceIntuit

Answer: Two semaphores — empty (free slots, starts at N) and full (items, starts at 0) — plus a lock for the queue itself. Producers wait on empty, consumers wait on full.

class BoundedBuffer<T> {
    private readonly Queue<T> _q = new();
    private readonly SemaphoreSlim _empty, _full = new(0);
    private readonly object _gate = new();
    public BoundedBuffer(int capacity) => _empty = new SemaphoreSlim(capacity);

    public async Task PutAsync(T item) {
        await _empty.WaitAsync();               // wait for a free slot
        lock (_gate) _q.Enqueue(item);
        _full.Release();                        // signal: one more item
    }
    public async Task<T> TakeAsync() {
        await _full.WaitAsync();                // wait for an item
        T item; lock (_gate) item = _q.Dequeue();
        _empty.Release();                       // signal: one more free slot
        return item;
    }
}

Q12Print odd and even numbers alternately using two threads.MicrosoftWalmartInfosysWipro

Answer: Shared counter + a monitor. Each thread waits while it is not its turn, prints, increments, and pulses the other.

int n = 1; const int Max = 10; object gate = new();
void Run(int parity) {                            // parity 1 = odd, 0 = even
    while (true) lock (gate) {
        while (n <= Max && n % 2 != parity) Monitor.Wait(gate);
        if (n > Max) { Monitor.PulseAll(gate); return; }
        Console.WriteLine($"{parity}: {n++}");
        Monitor.PulseAll(gate);
    }
}
var t1 = new Thread(() => Run(1)); var t2 = new Thread(() => Run(0));
t1.Start(); t2.Start(); t1.Join(); t2.Join();
⚠️ Always wait in a while loop (spurious wake-ups, and the condition may have changed). Pulse on exit so the other thread is not stuck forever.

Concurrency = dealing with many things at once (interleaving, even on 1 core — e.g. async I/O). Parallelism = doing many things at the same instant on multiple cores.
Q14Why use a thread pool?MicrosoftIntuit
Thread creation is expensive and unbounded threads exhaust memory/context-switch the CPU to death. Pools reuse threads and bound concurrency. Don't block pool threads on I/O — use async.
Q15Optimistic vs pessimistic locking?AmazonRazorpay
Pessimistic: lock first (SELECT … FOR UPDATE) — good under high contention. Optimistic: read a version, write WHERE version = v, retry on conflict — good when conflicts are rare.
Q16Dining philosophers — the fix?TCSInfosys

Q1Explain ACID with a bank transfer.🔥AmazonPayPalRazorpayTCSInfosys

Q3How does an index work? Clustered vs non-clustered?🔥MicrosoftAmazonSalesforceAccentureTCS
  • An index is a separate sorted structure (usually a B+ tree) mapping key → row location, so lookups are O(log n) page reads instead of a full table scan.
  • Clustered: the table rows themselves are stored in key order (one per table — usually the primary key in SQL Server / InnoDB).
  • Non-clustered (secondary): separate tree whose leaves point to the row (or to the clustered key → extra "key lookup").
  • Covering index: includes all columns the query needs → no lookup back to the table (INCLUDE columns).
  • Cost: slower writes (every insert/update maintains each index) and extra storage.
Q4When will the database NOT use my index?AmazonFlipkartCognizant
  • Function or cast on the column: WHERE YEAR(created_at) = 2025 → rewrite as a range.
  • Leading wildcard: LIKE '%son'.
  • Composite index (a, b, c) but filtering only on b — the left-most prefix rule.
  • Low selectivity (status with 2 values) — a scan is cheaper.
  • Implicit type conversion (varchar column compared to an int).
  • OR across different columns, stale statistics.
Q5An API is slow because of a query. How do you debug it?🔥WalmartInfosysTCSCognizant
  1. Measure first — APM trace / logs: is time in the DB, the network or the app?
  2. Read the plan — EXPLAIN ANALYZE / actual execution plan: table scans, key lookups, bad row estimates, sorts spilling to disk.
  3. Fix the query — add/adjust the index (composite in the right order, covering), remove SELECT *, make predicates sargable, paginate with keyset (WHERE id > last) not big OFFSETs.
  4. Fix the access pattern — N+1 queries from the ORM (use Include/joins/batching), add caching for hot reads.
  5. Verify — compare before/after latency (p95/p99) and check write cost of new indexes.
Q6Normalization 1NF → 3NF, and when to denormalize?TCSWipro
1NF: atomic values, no repeating groups. 2NF: no partial dependency on part of a composite key. 3NF: no transitive dependency (non-key → non-key). BCNF: every determinant is a key. Denormalize for read-heavy paths (reporting, feeds) and accept update complexity.
Q7SQL vs NoSQL — how do you choose?AmazonSwiggy
SQL: relations, joins, multi-row transactions, ad-hoc queries (payments, orders). NoSQL: known access patterns at huge scale, flexible schema, easy horizontal scale (key-value, wide-column for time series, document). Choose by access pattern + consistency need, not hype.
Q8DELETE vs TRUNCATE vs DROP?TCSInfosysCapgemini
DELETE: row by row, WHERE allowed, logged, fires triggers, can roll back. TRUNCATE: removes all rows by deallocating pages, resets identity, minimal logging. DROP: removes the table definition too.
Q9WHERE vs HAVING; UNION vs UNION ALL?AccentureCognizant
WHERE filters rows before GROUP BY; HAVING filters groups after aggregation. UNION removes duplicates (sort/hash cost); UNION ALL keeps them and is faster.

Q4Implement a rate limiter class (per user, N requests per second).🔥AtlassianUberAmazonRazorpay

Answer: Token bucket per user: capacity = burst, refill at rate r/sec, lazily computed on each call (no background timer). Thread-safe with a small lock per bucket.

public sealed class TokenBucket {
    private readonly double _capacity, _refillPerSec;
    private double _tokens;
    private long _last = Stopwatch.GetTimestamp();
    private readonly object _gate = new();

    public TokenBucket(double capacity, double refillPerSec) {
        _capacity = capacity; _refillPerSec = refillPerSec; _tokens = capacity;
    }
    public bool TryAcquire() {
        lock (_gate) {
            long now = Stopwatch.GetTimestamp();
            double elapsed = (now - _last) / (double)Stopwatch.Frequency;
            _tokens = Math.Min(_capacity, _tokens + elapsed * _refillPerSec);
            _last = now;
            if (_tokens < 1) return false;
            _tokens -= 1;
            return true;
        }
    }
}

public sealed class RateLimiter {
    private readonly ConcurrentDictionary<string, TokenBucket> _buckets = new();
    private readonly double _cap, _rate;
    public RateLimiter(double cap, double rate) { _cap = cap; _rate = rate; }
    public bool Allow(string userId) =>
        _buckets.GetOrAdd(userId, _ => new TokenBucket(_cap, _rate)).TryAcquire();
}

Consistency & data distribution

Q1Explain CAP — and why PACELC is the more useful version.🔥GoogleAmazonAtlassianAccenture

Answer: During a network Partition, a distributed store must choose Consistency (refuse/serve errors to stay correct) or Availability (answer, possibly stale). Partitions are not optional, so it's really CP vs AP when partitioned.

PACELC: if Partition → A or C; Else (normal operation) → Latency or Consistency. Example: DynamoDB/Cassandra default PA/EL (fast, eventually consistent, tunable); Spanner / traditional single-leader SQL PC/EC.

💡 Choose per feature: payments/inventory → consistency; likes, feeds, view counts → availability + eventual consistency.
Q2Leader-follower vs multi-leader vs leaderless replication; what is a quorum?AmazonUberWalmart
  • Single leader: writes to the leader, async/sync replication to followers. Simple; replica lag → stale reads (fix: read-your-writes by reading from the leader for a few seconds after a write).
  • Multi-leader: writes in several regions; must resolve conflicts (last-write-wins, CRDTs).
  • Leaderless (Dynamo style): write to W of N replicas, read from R; if R + W > N, reads overlap the latest write. Read-repair + hinted handoff fix divergence.
Q3How do you shard a database? What problems come with it?🔥MetaGoogleFlipkartRazorpay
  • Range (by date/ID range) — good range scans, hot spots on the newest range.
  • Hash (hash(key) mod N or consistent hashing) — even spread, no range scans; consistent hashing moves only ~1/N keys when nodes change.
  • Directory / lookup — flexible placement, extra hop + the directory must be HA.

Problems: cross-shard joins and transactions, hot keys (celebrity user → split key / add salt / cache), resharding (use many virtual shards from day one), global secondary indexes, uneven growth.

↪ Pick the shard key from the main access pattern: chat → conversation_id, orders → customer_id, multi-tenant SaaS → tenant_id.
Q4Vertical vs horizontal scaling?TCSInfosys
Vertical: bigger machine — simple, has a ceiling and a single point of failure. Horizontal: more machines — needs stateless services, load balancing, partitioned data; scales further and adds fault tolerance.
Q5Strong vs eventual consistency?AmazonAdobe
Strong: every read sees the latest committed write (linearizable). Eventual: replicas converge if writes stop. Middle grounds: read-your-writes, monotonic reads, causal consistency.
Q6Partitioning vs sharding vs replication?CognizantSalesforce
Partitioning splits data (can be within one server); sharding = partitions spread across servers; replication = copies of the same data for availability and read scaling. Real systems do both.
Q7How is a leader elected?GoogleUber
Consensus (Raft/Paxos): majority vote with terms; a leader needs a quorum, so a minority partition can't elect one. In practice use etcd/ZooKeeper leases and fencing tokens to stop a stale leader writing.

Q9Why use a message queue? Kafka vs RabbitMQ? Delivery guarantees?🔥UberAmazonWalmartPhonePe

  • Transactional outbox: write the business row and an "event" row in the same local transaction; a relay publishes outbox rows to Kafka → no "DB updated but event lost".
  • Plus idempotency keys everywhere, because retries happen.

Q1How does async/await work, and why does .Result cause deadlocks?🔥MicrosoftTCSInfosysAccentureGCCs
  • The compiler rewrites an async method into a state machine. At an await on an incomplete task, the method returns to its caller; the rest is registered as a continuation.
  • For I/O (HTTP, DB, file) no thread is blocked while waiting — the OS signals completion and a thread-pool thread resumes the continuation. That's why async scales servers.
  • The continuation resumes on the captured SynchronizationContext (UI thread, old ASP.NET) if there is one.
  • Deadlock: UI/old ASP.NET thread calls task.Result → blocks the only context thread → the continuation needs that same thread → both wait forever.
💡 Rules: "async all the way", never .Result/.Wait() on async code, ConfigureAwait(false) in library code. ASP.NET Core has no SynchronizationContext, but blocking still starves the thread pool.
↪ CPU-bound work → Task.Run (not for I/O). Many independent calls → await Task.WhenAll(...). Hot path returning cached values → ValueTask.
Q2Value types vs reference types; struct vs class; boxing.MicrosoftTCSCognizantCapgemini

Value types (int, struct, enum) hold data directly and are copied on assignment; reference types (class, string, arrays) hold a reference to a heap object. (Value types live inline — on the stack for locals, inside the object for fields.) Boxing wraps a value type in a heap object when treated as object/interface — allocation + GC cost (e.g. non-generic ArrayList).

Use a struct for small, immutable, value-like data (Point, Money) — ideally ≤ 16 bytes; otherwise class. record gives value-based equality for classes; record struct for structs.

Q3How does the .NET garbage collector work? IDisposable vs finalizer?🔥MicrosoftInfosysHCLTechGCCs
  • Generational, mark-and-compact: Gen0 (new, collected often, cheap) → survivors promoted to Gen1 → Gen2 (long-lived, collected rarely). Objects ≥ 85 KB go to the Large Object Heap (collected with Gen2, not compacted by default).
  • Workstation vs Server GC; background GC reduces pauses.
  • The GC handles memory only. For unmanaged resources (file handles, sockets, DB connections) implement IDisposable and use using for deterministic cleanup.
  • A finalizer (~Class) is a safety net that runs at an unknown time on the finalizer thread and delays collection — call GC.SuppressFinalize(this) in Dispose.
↪ Memory leaks in .NET: static collections growing, event handlers never unsubscribed, caches without size limits, HttpClient created per request (socket exhaustion → use IHttpClientFactory).
Q4IEnumerable vs IQueryable; what is deferred execution?TCSInfosysWiproSalesforce

IQueryable builds an expression tree that the provider (EF Core) translates to SQL — filters run in the database. IEnumerable runs in memory — calling .AsEnumerable() or .ToList() too early pulls the whole table. LINQ is deferred: the query runs when enumerated, and runs again on every enumeration (multiple-enumeration bug).

Q5DI lifetimes — and the "captive dependency" bug?GCCsAccenture
Transient (new each time), Scoped (one per HTTP request — e.g. DbContext), Singleton (one per app). Injecting a Scoped service into a Singleton captures it forever (stale DbContext, threading bugs). ASP.NET Core validates this in Development.
Q6ASP.NET Core middleware pipeline?TCSCognizant
Ordered chain of delegates; each can act before/after calling next() or short-circuit. Order matters: exception handling → HTTPS → static files → routing → CORS → authentication → authorization → endpoints.
Q7const vs readonly vs static readonly?InfosysWipro
const: compile-time, baked into callers (changing it needs recompiling dependents). readonly: set in the constructor, per instance. static readonly: set once at type init, runtime value (e.g. a TimeSpan).
Q8Why is string immutable; when StringBuilder?TCSCapgemini
Immutability gives thread safety, safe hashing as dictionary keys and interning. Each concatenation allocates a new string → in loops use StringBuilder (or string.Join/interpolation for a few parts).
Q9EF Core performance tips?GCCsWalmart
AsNoTracking() for reads, project with Select to DTOs, avoid N+1 (Include / split queries), paginate, compiled queries for hot paths, batch saves, check generated SQL in logs.
Q10How does Dictionary work internally?MicrosoftInfosys
Array of buckets; GetHashCode() picks a bucket, collisions chained; resizes (≈ doubling to a prime) when full → amortised O(1). Override both Equals and GetHashCode for custom keys; never mutate a key's hashed fields.

Q12Closures — and the classic var-in-a-loop bug.🔥MetaAdobeInfosysCognizant 
for (var i = 0; i < 3; i++) setTimeout(() => console.log(i), 0);   // 3 3 3
for (let i = 0; i < 3; i++) setTimeout(() => console.log(i), 0);   // 0 1 2

Answer: A closure is a function bundled with references to the variables of the scope where it was created. var is function-scoped → one shared i (3 when the timers run). let is block-scoped → a fresh binding per iteration. Uses: data privacy (module pattern), memoisation, partial application, React hooks.

Q13Implement debounce. How is throttle different?MetaGoogleFlipkartAtlassian 
function debounce(fn, wait) {
  let timer;
  return function (...args) {
    clearTimeout(timer);
    timer = setTimeout(() => fn.apply(this, args), wait);
  };
}
const onSearch = debounce(q => fetchSuggestions(q), 300);

Debounce: run once after the calls stop for wait ms (search box). Throttle: run at most once every wait ms while calls continue (scroll / resize handlers).

Q14var vs let vs const; hoisting; TDZ?TCSAccenture

Q15How is "this" decided?MetaAdobe

Q16Promise.all vs allSettled vs race vs any?AtlassianInfosys

Q17Node is single-threaded — how does it handle 10K connections?UberPayPalWalmart

Q18== vs ===; prototypal inheritance?TCSWipro
== coerces types (0 == '' is true); === compares without coercion — always use it. Objects delegate missing properties up the [[Prototype]] chain; class is syntax sugar over prototypes.

Q1Container vs virtual machine.🔥TCSInfosysAccentureSalesforce

Q7Explain Kubernetes architecture — what happens on kubectl apply?🔥MicrosoftSalesforceGCCsAccenture
  • Control plane: kube-apiserver (the only door; validates, stores), etcd (consistent key-value store of desired + current state), scheduler (picks a node for unscheduled pods), controller-manager (reconciliation loops: Deployment, ReplicaSet, Node…), cloud-controller-manager.

💡 Everything is a reconciliation loop: controllers keep moving actual state toward desired state.
Q8Liveness vs readiness vs startup probes.🔥MicrosoftWalmartGCCs
  • Readiness fails → pod removed from Service endpoints (no traffic), not restarted. Use for warm-up, dependency outages, draining.
  • Liveness fails → kubelet restarts the container. Use only for "stuck/deadlocked" detection.
  • Startup → disables the others until the app has started (slow-booting apps).
⚠️ Liveness that checks the database → DB blip restarts every pod at once (cascading failure). Keep liveness shallow; put dependencies in readiness.
Q9A pod is in CrashLoopBackOff. How do you debug it?🔥TCSInfosysGCCsPayPal

Q10Requests vs limits; how does autoscaling work?AmazonMicrosoftFlipkart

Q11Deployment vs StatefulSet vs DaemonSet vs Job?GCCsSalesforce

Q1Supervised vs unsupervised vs reinforcement learning?TCSInfosys
Supervised: labelled examples → predict (spam, price). Unsupervised: find structure without labels (clustering, anomaly detection). RL: agent learns from rewards (games, robotics; RLHF tunes LLMs on human preferences).
Q2Overfitting vs underfitting?AmazonAccenture
Overfit: great on train, poor on test (high variance) → more data, regularisation (L1/L2, dropout), early stopping, simpler model. Underfit: poor on both (high bias) → richer features/model, train longer.
Q3Precision vs recall — which matters when?GooglePayPal
Precision = of predicted positives, how many are right (spam filter: don't bin real mail). Recall = of real positives, how many we caught (cancer screening, fraud). F1 = harmonic mean. Accuracy misleads on imbalanced data.
Q4How does a neural network learn?InfosysCapgemini
Forward pass → loss → backpropagation computes gradients via the chain rule → gradient descent (SGD/Adam) nudges weights against the gradient by the learning rate. Repeat over mini-batches/epochs.

LLMs

Q5Explain how a Transformer / LLM works in 2 minutes.🔥GoogleMicrosoftMetaAdobe
  1. Text → tokens (sub-words) → embeddings (vectors) + position information.
  2. Each layer uses self-attention: every token builds a Query, Key and Value; attention weights = softmax(Q·Kᵀ / √d); the output mixes Values — so each token "looks at" the tokens most relevant to it. Multi-head = several such views in parallel. Then a feed-forward network.
  3. Decoder-only LLMs are trained to predict the next token on huge text corpora (causal mask: can't see the future), then instruction-tuned and preference-tuned (RLHF/DPO).
  4. Generation = sample one token at a time; temperature/top-p control randomness.
↪ Why better than RNNs? Parallel training over the whole sequence and direct long-range connections (no vanishing gradients across steps). Cost: attention is O(n²) in sequence length → context-window limits, KV cache at inference.
Q6What are embeddings and how does vector search work?AmazonAtlassianSalesforce

Answer: An embedding model maps text (or images) to a vector so that similar meanings are close (cosine similarity / dot product). Vector stores (pgvector, Azure AI Search, OpenSearch, Pinecone…) use approximate nearest neighbour indexes such as HNSW or IVF to find top-k neighbours in milliseconds among millions of vectors, trading a little recall for speed. Combine with keyword search (BM25) = hybrid search.

Q1Rotate Image (n×n, 90° clockwise, in place).🔥MicrosoftAmazonAdobeTCS 
int n = m.Length;
for (int i = 0; i < n; i++)
    for (int j = i + 1; j < n; j++)                 // upper triangle only
        (m[i][j], m[j][i]) = (m[j][i], m[i][j]);

⚠️ Starting j at 0 swaps every pair twice and undoes the transpose.
Q2Spiral Matrix — return elements in spiral order.MicrosoftAmazonGoogleInfosys

Answer: Keep four walls top, bottom, left, right. Walk right along top (top++), down the right wall (right--), then — only if top ≤ bottom — left along bottom (bottom--), and only if left ≤ right — up the left wall (left++). Repeat while top ≤ bottom and left ≤ right. O(m·n).

⚠️ The two "only if" checks prevent double-printing the middle row/column of non-square matrices.
Q3Set Matrix Zeroes with O(1) extra space.MetaMicrosoftWalmart

Answer: Use the first row and first column as marker arrays. First remember (two booleans) whether row 0 / column 0 themselves contain a zero. Mark m[i][0] = m[0][j] = 0 for every zero cell, then zero cells (i, j ≥ 1) whose row or column marker is 0, and finally zero row 0 / column 0 if the booleans say so. O(m·n) time, O(1) space.

Q4Search a row- and column-sorted 2D matrix?AmazonMicrosoft
Start at the top-right: bigger than target → move left; smaller → move down. Each step discards a row or column → O(m + n). (Fully sorted "row continues the previous" matrix → one binary search over m·n.)
Q5Game of Life in place?GoogleAtlassian
Encode old and new state in the same cell: e.g. 2 = "was alive, now dead", 3 = "was dead, now alive"; count neighbours using the old state (value % 2 or a check for 1/2), then a second pass maps 2→0, 3→1.

Bit manipulation

Q6Single Number — every element appears twice except one.🔥AmazonAdobeTCSWipro

Answer: XOR everything: a ^ a = 0, a ^ 0 = a, and XOR is commutative → the pairs cancel. O(n) / O(1).

↪ Every other number appears three times → count each bit position mod 3. Two singles → XOR all to get x^y, split numbers by any set bit of that result, XOR each group.
Q7Counting Bits — number of 1s for every i in 0..n.AmazonAppleAdobe 
var bits = new int[n + 1];
for (int i = 1; i <= n; i++) bits[i] = bits[i >> 1] + (i & 1);   // drop last bit, add it back

Alternative: bits[i] = bits[i & (i - 1)] + 1. Both O(n).

Q8Missing Number in 0..n?MicrosoftInfosysCognizant
XOR all indices 0..n with all values — the missing one survives. Or n(n+1)/2 − sum (watch overflow → long). Sorting is O(n log n) and not needed.
Q9Generate all subsets with bitmasks?MetaUber
For mask 0..2ⁿ−1, element i is in the subset if (mask >> i) & 1. O(n·2ⁿ). Same idea powers bitmask DP (TSP-style, n ≤ 20).
Q10Add two integers without + or −?MetaApple
Sum without carry = a ^ b; carry = (a & b) << 1. Repeat with a = sum, b = carry until carry is 0. Works for negatives in two's complement with fixed-width ints.
Q11Useful bit one-liners?TCSAccenture
Check bit: (n >> i) & 1 · set: n | (1 << i) · clear: n & ~(1 << i) · toggle: n ^ (1 << i) · lowest set bit: n & -n · odd: (n & 1) == 1.

String puzzles

Q12Find a pattern in text in O(n + m) — explain KMP.GoogleMicrosoftAdobe

Answer: Precompute lps[i] = length of the longest proper prefix of pattern[0..i] that is also a suffix. On a mismatch, instead of restarting, jump the pattern pointer to lps[j-1] — the text pointer never moves backwards.

int[] lps = new int[p.Length];
for (int i = 1, len = 0; i < p.Length; ) {
    if (p[i] == p[len]) lps[i++] = ++len;
    else if (len > 0) len = lps[len - 1];
    else lps[i++] = 0;
}
for (int i = 0, j = 0; i < s.Length; ) {
    if (s[i] == p[j]) { i++; j++; if (j == p.Length) return i - j; }
    else if (j > 0) j = lps[j - 1];
    else i++;
}
return -1;                                     // (empty pattern → return 0 up front)
↪ Alternatives to name: Rabin-Karp (rolling hash, great for many patterns / plagiarism), Z-algorithm. In production: IndexOf(…, StringComparison.Ordinal).
Q13Decode String: "3[a2[c]]" → "accaccacc".🔥GoogleAmazonAtlassianFlipkart

Answer: Two stacks: counts and partial strings. Digit → build k (multi-digit!). [ → push (k, current), reset. ] → pop (k, prev), current = prev + current × k. Letter → append. Use StringBuilder. Time = O(output length).

Q14Basic Calculator II ("3+2*2", no parentheses).MetaAmazonUber

Answer: Keep the running result and the last term; + and − commit the last term, * and / modify it (precedence for free). O(n) time, O(1) space.

int result = 0, last = 0, num = 0; char op = '+';
for (int i = 0; i <= s.Length; i++) {
    char c = i < s.Length ? s[i] : '+';          // sentinel flushes the last number
    if (char.IsDigit(c)) { num = num * 10 + (c - '0'); continue; }
    if (c == ' ') continue;
    if (op == '+')      { result += last; last = num; }
    else if (op == '-') { result += last; last = -num; }
    else if (op == '*') last *= num;
    else                last /= num;              // truncates toward zero, as required
    op = c; num = 0;
}
return result + last;
↪ With parentheses (Calculator I/III) → recursion on "(" or a stack of (result, sign) frames.
Q15String to Integer (atoi) — what edge cases?MicrosoftAmazonTCS
Leading spaces, optional single sign, stop at first non-digit, empty/only-sign input → 0, overflow → clamp to int.MinValue/MaxValue (check before multiplying: res > (int.MaxValue - d) / 10).
Q16Valid Palindrome II (delete at most one char)?MetaInfosys
Two pointers; at the first mismatch, return isPal(L+1..R) || isPal(L..R−1). O(n). Meta's #1 warm-up for years.
Q17Reorganize String (no two equal neighbours)?AmazonGoogle
Impossible if max frequency > (n+1)/2. Else max-heap by count: take the top, append, hold it back one round. Or fill even indices with the most frequent letter first, then the rest. O(n log k).
Q18Reverse words in a string in place?TCSWiproCapgemini
Reverse the whole char array, then reverse each word; squeeze extra spaces with a write pointer. O(n), O(1) extra if the language allows mutable strings (char[] in C#).

Q1Sliding Window Maximum in O(n).🔥GoogleAmazonAtlassianUber

Answer: Deque of indices whose values are decreasing. For each i: drop the front if it left the window; pop the back while its value ≤ nums[i] (they can never be a max again); push i; the front is the window max. Each index enters and leaves once → O(n).

var dq = new LinkedList<int>(); var res = new List<int>();
for (int i = 0; i < nums.Length; i++) {

    dq.AddLast(i);
    if (i >= k - 1) res.Add(nums[dq.First.Value]);

Q8Regular Expression Matching with '.' and '*'.GoogleMetaUber

Answer: dp[i][j] = does s[0..i) match p[0..j). If p[j−1] is '*': zero copies → dp[i][j−2], or one more copy → dp[i−1][j] when s[i−1] matches p[j−2]. Else: dp[i−1][j−1] when chars match (or p is '.'). Base: dp[0][0] = true; dp[0][j] = p[j−1]=='*' && dp[0][j−2] (patterns like "a*b*" match empty). O(m·n).

Q9Burst Balloons — the "interval DP" template.GoogleMicrosoft

Answer: Pad with 1s at both ends. Think about the balloon burst last in (l, r): its neighbours are then l and r, and the two sides are independent. dp[l][r] = max over k of dp[l][k] + dp[k][r] + a[l]·a[k]·a[r], filled by increasing interval length. O(n³).

💡 "Choose the last action, not the first" turns dependent sub-problems into independent ones. Same for Matrix-Chain Multiplication and Minimum Cost to Cut a Stick.
Q10Longest Valid Parentheses?AmazonMeta
Stack of indices seeded with −1. "(" → push i. ")" → pop; if empty push i (new base) else length = i − top. O(n). Or two counter passes (left→right, right→left), O(1) space.
Q11Longest Increasing Path in a Matrix?GoogleMeta
DFS from every cell with memo[r][c] = 1 + max over strictly larger neighbours. "Strictly increasing" means no cycles, so memoised DFS is safe. O(m·n).

Q1Implement a limit-order matching engine (price-time priority).🔥PhonePeZerodha / fintechUberbank GCCs 
enum Side { Buy, Sell }
record Order(long Id, Side Side, decimal Price, int Qty, long Seq);
record Trade(long BuyId, long SellId, decimal Price, int Qty);

class OrderBook {
    // priority = (price key, arrival seq): bids use -price so the highest bid comes first
    private readonly PriorityQueue<Order, (decimal, long)> _bids = new(), _asks = new();

    public List<Trade> Place(Order o) {
        var trades = new List<Trade>();
        var opposite = o.Side == Side.Buy ? _asks : _bids;
        int remaining = o.Qty;
        while (remaining > 0 && opposite.TryPeek(out var best, out _) && Crosses(o, best)) {
            opposite.Dequeue();
            int fill = Math.Min(remaining, best.Qty);
            trades.Add(o.Side == Side.Buy ? new Trade(o.Id, best.Id, best.Price, fill)
                                          : new Trade(best.Id, o.Id, best.Price, fill));
            remaining -= fill;
            if (best.Qty > fill) opposite.Enqueue(best with { Qty = best.Qty - fill }, Key(best));  // keeps its Seq
        }
        if (remaining > 0) {
            var rest = o with { Qty = remaining };
            (o.Side == Side.Buy ? _bids : _asks).Enqueue(rest, Key(rest));
        }
        return trades;
    }

    static bool Crosses(Order o, Order best) => o.Side == Side.Buy ? o.Price >= best.Price : o.Price <= best.Price;
    static (decimal, long) Key(Order o) => (o.Side == Side.Buy ? -o.Price : o.Price, o.Seq);
}
↪ Extensions they ask: cancel (lazy-delete set checked on peek, or SortedDictionary<price, LinkedList<Order>> + id → node map for O(1) cancel), market orders (no price check), one book per symbol, single-threaded matcher per symbol fed by a queue (no locks, deterministic).

Q4Tic-tac-toe on an n×n board — check the winner in O(1) per move.MicrosoftAmazonInfosys 
int[] rows = new int[n], cols = new int[n]; int diag = 0, anti = 0;
int Move(int r, int c, int player) {           // player 1 → +1, player 2 → -1
    int v = player == 1 ? 1 : -1;
    rows[r] += v; cols[c] += v;
    if (r == c) diag += v;
    if (r + c == n - 1) anti += v;
    if (Math.Abs(rows[r]) == n || Math.Abs(cols[c]) == n || Math.Abs(diag) == n || Math.Abs(anti) == n)
        return player;
    return 0;
}

  • Generation metrics: faithfulness/groundedness (every claim supported by retrieved text), answer relevance, correctness vs reference, citation accuracy — scored by human review plus an LLM-as-judge calibrated against humans.
  • Online: thumbs up/down, escalation-to-human rate, deflection rate, latency and cost per answer.
  • Run evals in CI on every prompt/model/chunking change — prompts are code.
Q10Chunking strategies — and why chunking matters so much.GoogleAdobeAccenture

Too small → chunks lose context; too large → noisy retrieval and wasted tokens. Options: fixed size with overlap (baseline), structure-aware (split on headings/sections, keep tables whole), semantic (split where topic shifts), parent-child (retrieve small chunks, send the larger parent section to the model). Attach metadata (title, section path, date, ACL) and prepend the document title to each chunk.

Q11LoRA / QLoRA / quantization?MetaGoogle
LoRA trains small low-rank adapter matrices instead of all weights → cheap fine-tuning, swappable adapters. QLoRA does it on a 4-bit quantized base model to fit on smaller GPUs. Quantization (8/4-bit) shrinks memory and speeds inference at a small quality cost.
Q12What drives LLM latency?MicrosoftSwiggy
Time-to-first-token (prompt length → prefill) + output tokens ÷ tokens/sec (decode). Cut it with shorter prompts, prompt caching, smaller models for easy steps, streaming to the UI, parallel tool calls, capped output length.
Q13How do you get reliable structured output?IntuitAtlassian
Use the provider's structured-output / tool-calling with a JSON schema, validate in code (strong types), retry with the validation error on failure, keep fields minimal, and never trust the output for authorization decisions.
Q14Estimate the cost of an LLM feature?AmazonFlipkart
requests/day × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price), plus embeddings and vector-store costs. E.g. 100K requests × (3K in + 300 out tokens). Then show levers: caching hit-rate, smaller model routing, fewer retrieved chunks.
Q15Long conversations exceed the context window?GoogleSalesforce
Keep the system prompt + recent turns verbatim, summarise older turns, store facts in retrievable memory (vector store / profile), and retrieve only what's relevant to the current turn.
Q16When should you NOT use an agent?MetaAtlassian
When the steps are known — a fixed workflow (retrieve → generate → validate) is cheaper, faster, testable and predictable. Use agents when the path genuinely depends on intermediate results, and add limits + human approval for actions.
Q17You switch embedding models — what breaks?AmazonAdobe
Vectors from different models aren't comparable → re-embed the whole corpus into a new index, dual-run and compare retrieval evals, then cut over (blue/green indexes). Store the model name/version with each vector.
Q18What guardrails would you put on a customer-facing bot?PayPalbank GCCs
Input: PII redaction, prompt-injection and abuse classifiers, topic limits. Output: grounding/citation checks, toxicity filter, no financial/medical advice rules, safe refusals. Plus rate limits, audit logs and a human-handoff path.
🎯 Takeaway
For stories: scope, trade-off, number, learning. For GenAI: retrieval quality, evals, cost/latency levers, guardrails — say those four and you sound like someone who has shipped it.
